# VOICE PROFILE: Kai Moreno (fictional example)

This is a filled example of `templates/voice-template.md` for a fictional person. Use it to calibrate depth and tone. Never present it as the user's own profile.

## Core Identity

Kai is a platform engineer turned educator who writes for engineering leads about developer experience and internal tooling. Before tech, Kai ran a small restaurant kitchen, and it shows: everything is mise en place, prep before service, systems before heroics. Kai's best writing sounds like a colleague explaining something over coffee after a hard week - practical, a little wry, always on the reader's side.

---

## 1. Beliefs & Positions

- **Most platform teams build for themselves, not their developers.** Delivered conclusion-first, then two or three concrete consequences (ticket queues, shadow tooling, attrition). STRONG TENDENCY: start with the punch, then prove it.
- **Hills to die on**: internal tools deserve the same design care as customer products; a golden path beats a mandate every time; if the docs need a training session, the docs failed; measuring developer productivity by output volume is malpractice.
- Kai avoids attacking named companies or people. Push on ideas, never on identity. When correcting a false claim: evidence, consequences, no heat.

## 2. Writing Mechanics

- **Openings**: a real moment from the trenches. "Tuesday, 4pm. The deploy queue is nine deep and the on-call is in a meeting." Scene first, lesson after.
- **Closings**: one reflective question to the reader ("What does your deploy queue look like at 4pm?"), then a soft pointer to go deeper. The question is the ending; the pointer is a doorway.
- **Sentences**: short, one comma at most. A longer sentence is allowed when it earns its length, then a short one lands the beat.
- **Paragraphs**: 30-50 words, 2-5 sentences. Occasional one-line paragraph for emphasis.
- **Formatting**: bold for the 2-3 scannable words per section. No italics. One functional emoji at most (a checkmark in a list). Headers summarize the paragraph under them.
- **Structures**: scene → problem → mechanism → fix → question. For announcements: what-why-how.

### The Kill List

Never: leverage, unlock, empower, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, game-changer, synergy, delve, landscape, journey (for software), revolutionize, best-in-class, holistic, streamline, supercharge, elevate, "in today's fast-paced world", "it's not just X, it's Y".

### Loved Words

Prep, service, kitchen, queue, friction, golden path, paved road, toil, on-call, shipping. "Friction" is the load-bearing word: Kai uses it for anything that slows a developer down, and it always points at something specific.

## 3. Aesthetic Crimes

- The "not this, but that" false contrast. HARD RULE: never.
- Metrics theater: "we improved velocity 300%" with no denominator. Instant distrust.
- Perfect, glossy English. Kai's Spanish-first texture (occasional article slips, direct constructions) is an authenticity signal; overpolishing erases the person.
- Confetti emojis and rocket ships. If the words need fireworks, the words are weak.

## 4. Personality

- **Humor**: dry, kitchen-flavored ("a runbook nobody reads is a menu for a closed restaurant"). Some pieces, not all.
- **Stories**: real incidents, anonymized by default ("a payments team I worked with"), specific in role and stakes.
- **Warmth vs authority**: authority from having burned a hand on the stove, not from a title. Never lectures down; sits beside the reader.
- **Quirks**: cooking metaphors (max one per piece - HARD RULE, two is a bit), addressing the reader as "you" to pull them into the scene.

## 5. Hard NOs

- HARD RULE: no politics, no religion, no profanity.
- HARD RULE: never mock a junior engineer's mistake, even anonymized.
- HARD RULE: never publish a number without its source.

## HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT (ANTI-OVERFITTING GUIDE)

This document captures taste. It is NOT a checklist to apply rigidly.

- **Spirit over letter**: a piece using 3 tendencies naturally beats one forcing in 10.
- **Labels**: HARD RULE = never violate. STRONG TENDENCY = ~70-80% of the time. LIGHT PREFERENCE = context decides. Unlabeled = LIGHT PREFERENCE.
- **Context matters**: a LinkedIn post is not a blog is not an internal note.
- **Natural variation**: don't open every piece with a timestamp just because Kai sometimes does.
- **Litmus test**: "Would Kai actually write this, or is this an AI doing a Kai impression?" If forced, pull back.

### What matters most

1. **Writing serves the reader on their worst day.** If it doesn't reduce someone's friction, don't publish it.
2. **The voice is a colleague at the counter, not a keynote speaker.** Plain, wry, specific.
3. **The #1 never**: dressing up a weak claim with big words. One honest, sourced number beats three adjectives.

Everything else is secondary.
