# Point-of-view structure

A PoV takes a position and defends it in 2-4 pages. The reader should be able to disagree with it - that is what makes it worth reading. No cover page; the title block leads straight into the stance.

Skeleton:

1. **The stance** - open with the position in the first two sentences. No wind-up, no market context first.
2. **Why now** - what changed that makes this position urgent. One paragraph.
3. **The argument** - exactly three supporting arguments, each a level-2 subsection with evidence from the wiki. Three is the discipline: a fourth argument means one of the first three is weak.
4. **What we would do** - the implication for the reader, concrete. This is where SUSE (or the author's product) may enter.
5. **The honest caveat** - the strongest counterargument, stated fairly, and why the stance survives it.

Rules of the form:

- First person plural is allowed ("we believe"); hedging is not ("it could be argued").
- One figure if the argument has structure worth drawing; zero is fine.
- One callout max: the stance itself, restated mid-document.
- 600-2200 words. Over that, it is a whitepaper wearing the wrong coat.
