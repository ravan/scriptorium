# Whitepaper structure

A whitepaper educates a technical evaluator toward a conclusion. It is evidence-led, vendor-restrained, and every claim traces to the wiki.

Required skeleton (each top-level item is a `heading` level-1 section):

1. **Executive summary** - the whole argument in 150-250 words. Written last, placed first. A busy reader stops here and still gets the point.
2. **The problem** - the situation and why it hurts now. Name the reader's world, not the product.
3. **The approach** - the idea that resolves the problem, stated before any product detail. One figure showing the concept.
4. **How it works** - the substance. 2-4 level-2 subsections, each with a figure where structure exists (architecture, flow, comparison). This is where depth lives.
5. **Evidence** - numbers, cases, reference results. Real figures beat adjectives; use the wiki's data.
6. **Conclusion and next steps** - restate the argument in new words, then one concrete action for the reader.
7. **References** - numbered list of cited sources (wiki raw sources, public documents).

Rules of the form:

- Product names enter at section 4, not before.
- A figure every 2-3 pages; generate SVGs per the brand skill, never screenshot-quality raster.
- Callouts carry the one-sentence takeaway of a long section - max one per section.
- Bullets are legal but paragraphs argue better; a bullet list longer than 5 items is a table pretending to be prose.
- 1500-6000 words. Under 1500, write a point-of-view instead.
